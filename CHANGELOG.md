# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Fixed

- **A unit on its way into or out of a tray takes no tap** (decision 0023). Right after a tap, the unit just put in the tray was still moving over the spot where the next loose unit now lay, so a quick second tap there took it back out of the tray (seen in Learn's browser test on CI, where two of ten quick taps were undone). A unit whose tray changes is marked moving, with no pointer events, until its move ends (420 ms); a tap there reaches the unit underneath. The browser check taps at the same spot at once and checks which unit is hit.

### Added

- **The trading mat speaks Chinese** (math plugin 0.6.0; decision 0023 amendment 3). A `base_ten_trade` card takes an optional `language`, `en-US` or `zh-CN`; without it the mat speaks US English as before, so existing lessons are unchanged byte for byte and still require math 0.5.0. A Chinese mat's headings, tray labels, buttons, units' spoken labels and refused-tap messages are whole Chinese sentences (`BASE_TEN_WORDS`), with numbers written in characters, so the card still draws no digit; the layout is the same in both languages. A lesson with a mat that names its language requires `plugin:math/base-ten-trade-language@1` (math 0.6.0). `baseTenRefusalText` takes the language; the runtime passes the mat's. The browser check adds a Chinese mat and a Chinese card task.

### Fixed

- **"I'm done" with a few ones left in the tray succeeds** (decision 0023 amendment 2; found by a first-use test in Learn). `no_trade_left` no longer requires empty trays, only that no place below the highest holds ten or more; a full tray already makes its place ten or more, so no trade can be skipped. The refused-trade message states a fact ("This tray does not hold a ten: a ten is ten ones.") instead of "Fill the tray first", which was impossible with fewer than ten left. After "done" the mat is laid tidy, trays empty, with the same counts.
- **Question cards on one card stack in their order** (teaching layout; decision 0023). A question card opened directly under its anchor and pushed earlier ones down, so several on one card read bottom to top, below the practice task. A card now opens under the cards that hang directly under the same anchor (in its column, stacked without a gap). A card with nothing else under its anchor is placed exactly as before; a question on a card whose practice task hangs directly under it now opens under that task.
- **A fraction model's marked share takes one piece of each part, and the card writes the number's other form** (math plugin 0.4.2; decision 0021 amendment 2). A highlight of *n* smallest parts now marks one piece in each shaded part before a second in any: half of 3/5 marks one half of each fifth, and two wholes shared by three mark one third of each whole (it ran from the left, so both thirds sat in the first whole). Beside the stacked label the card writes the same number in another form, from the values: a whole or mixed number on bars and circles once the parts fill a whole ("= 2 1/3", "= 4"), and a decimal on grids of tenths or hundredths ("= 0.25", "= 1.6"); a bar of tenths gets none, so a fraction lesson gets no decimal. Circles and grids shrink when that column is kept, so a row of wholes stays clear of the labels; the column is kept for every value of a bound numerator, so the card keeps one size, and a fixed fraction that never fills a whole keeps the full width. The browser check adds a card revised with its cuts (the two steps of the amendment), a revised card that grows without running into the card below it, labels clear of every drawing, a slider's label that stays put, and the forms. Lessons still require math 0.4.0.
- **Fraction grids fill column by column** (math plugin 0.4.1; decision 0021). A grid's shaded cells ran row by row from the top left; they now run down each column, left to right, as US hundred grids show tenths (0.43 is four full columns and three cells). Lesson narration in both fraction probes (Coach `docs/FRACTION_PROBE_2026-11-19.md`) described columns under a picture shaded by rows. Bars and circles are unchanged; lessons still require math 0.4.0.
- **Function-plot marks hide at a coefficient that cancels to a rounding residue** (math plan 3.6). A sum of lesson-number terms can come out as 5.6e-17 instead of 0 (0.1·n − 0.3 at n = 3), so a vertex, axis or x-intercept that hides at a = 0 was drawn far off the plot instead; and a double root whose discriminant came out as −2.2e-16 was hidden. `markParts` now multiplies a divisor that is a sum by an exact 0-or-1 factor, `1 − floor(1/(1 + floor(|v|·1e12)))`, so it is exactly 0 below 1e-12 and exactly itself otherwise; the discriminant gets the same factor relative to its own size (b² + |4ac|), so quadratics with tiny coefficients keep their roots (review). A constant or a single product (an exponent's sign is not a sum) is written as before. A part the factor would push past 240 characters is written as before, never left out: in practice the vertex and axis always fit, and the x-intercepts fit when the coefficients are short. Tests: the residue vertex, the double root, tiny coefficients, products left alone.

Ported from the accepted sketch v3 and its tests.

    - a tray of ten slots between each place and the next, labelled ("Ten ones make a ten"), with "Make a ten" and "Break a ten" under it;
    - ones, tens and hundreds to scale, with hundreds three to a row and never overlapping.

    Every tappable element is marked `data-oll-tap`. Taps, the learner's "done" task and the demonstration's tap event come in the next pull requests; until then the card only draws.

- **Function-plot derivations in plugin-math** (decision 0018; shared by Coach and Studio): `markParts` (intercepts, vertex, symmetry axis), `formulaBars` and `pieceBars` (domain and range), `intervalOf` and `signOf` (signs proved over variable ranges), on postfix `PlotToken` formulas. `plotFormulaTokens` parses a formula by the grammar of the expression compiler (a 20,000-case fuzz agreed exactly), keeping the caller's variable names; `plotFormulaText` writes tokens back. - Function plot parts for decision 0018 (player 0.11.0):
  - **Pieces of a piecewise function** (`plot:curve-intervals`): a `y = f(x)` curve may set `from` and `to` (either side may be left unbounded) and `from_closed` / `to_closed`. It is drawn only on its interval, with a filled dot at a closed end and a ring at an open one; where they meet the filled dot is on top, and an end with no finite value gets no dot. Pieces of one function share a `series` name, so they share one colour, dash and legend entry, and are shown or hidden together in the explorer. Pieces of one function may meet only at an end that at most one of them includes; overlapping pieces are refused.
  - **Intervals on the axes** (`plot:axis-intervals`): `intervals` with `axis: "x" | "y"`, `from` and `to` (finite or null), and closed or open ends, to mark a domain or a range. They are drawn like the number line's intervals, on the zero axis or on the frame edge when 0 is outside the window, with arrowheads for unbounded ends. Their ends can be bound to variables.
  - **Hidden while undefined:** `hide_when_undefined` now also applies to plot guide values (`plot:guide-hide-undefined`, for example the symmetry axis of a parabola that flattens) and interval ends. A binding with the option may also start undefined, for example the x-intercepts of a parabola that starts above the axis.
  - **Slope triangle:** the legs of a secant measurement are named Δx and Δy. When every curve the two points lie on is a straight line, the readout says "斜率" (slope) rather than "割线斜率" (secant slope). The measurement uses the first two points, so marks added after them leave it in place (`plot:secant-first-two`, since an older player drew no triangle with more than two points), and point labels that would collide take another spot around their point.
  - Browser check: `npm run check:plot-browser`.
- Several jumps on one number line (decision 0017; `plugin:math/number-line-jump-lanes@1`, math plugin 0.3.0):
  - A jump whose span overlaps an earlier jump's (up 4 then down 6; up 6, down 4, up 3) is drawn on a raised lane, with dashed leaders down to its start and its end and the arrow on the end's leader, so no arc crosses another and each label stays over its own arc. Chained jumps in one direction only touch at their ends and stay on the line.
  - A raised jump is one arc even with `hops: true`; hops stay on the line's own lane.
  - A moving card (bound or dragged) keeps its line in place: it reserves the lanes its variables can reach, sampled at every stop a variable can rest on (its slider step, or the smallest tick for a point dragged on the card) when there are few enough, else at its ends, its start and an even grid between them (about a thousand states in all). An overlap narrower than both a stop and a grid step, crossed between stops in an animation, can still be missed; the card then grows while it lasts.
  - Lessons whose jumps overlap as written, and moving cards with several jumps, require the feature, so a player with the math plugin 0.2.0 refuses them instead of drawing crossing arcs. Other number lines need only `plugin:math/number-line@1` and 0.2.0, as before.

- Number line (decision 0013), the first card of a subject plugin: kind `number_line` in `plugin-math` (math plugin 0.2.0).
  - Content: a range and ticks (major step and subdivisions; labels as integers, decimals, or stacked fractions such as 3/4, which are not reduced, or mixed numbers; decimals keep the places the step needs, so quarters read 0.25 and 0.75; when labels would overlap they thin out on a regular stride, 0 keeps its label, and every tick stays); points at numbers or exact fractions (`"3/4"`, `"1 1/2"`), closed or open, optionally shown only when emphasized; jumps from a point or a value, drawn as an arc or one hop per smallest tick, whose end the program computes (a variable may stop a hop-by-hop jump between ticks; its last hop is drawn shorter so the arrow still ends there); and intervals with open, closed or unbounded ends.
  - Points, jumps and intervals are addressable parts, and their values can be bound to lesson variables and animated. A bound number line is checked with each variable at its minimum, maximum and starting value, so no reachable value carries a part off the line. Integer labels go only on integer ticks, and integer labels on every tick of a subdivided line are rejected.
  - A learner can drag a point (`interaction: {kind: "drag", variable}`, control `number_line_point`). It snaps to the smallest tick, and `expression_target` judges it. A drag task whose target lies between ticks is rejected as unreachable.
  - First-party plugins ship with OLL and are installed by default (`FIRST_PARTY_EXTENSIONS`; `firstParty: false` leaves them out). A number line requires `plugin:math/number-line@1` and the math plugin 0.2.0, not a core node feature, so a player without it refuses the lesson before playback. Lessons without a number line need no plugin and derive the same requirements as before.
  - `buildNumberLine` builds the card for Coach and Studio. The example `learner-context-number-line-v2` ("3 + (−5)") replaces the seven boxes that faked a number line with the real card; its move is five hops from 3 to −2. Teaching observation: `npm run teaching:observe:number-line`.
